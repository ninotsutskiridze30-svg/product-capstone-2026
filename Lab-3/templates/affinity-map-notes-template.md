## The Problem Statement

> University students in Georgia who need a private tutor spend between several hours and several days finding one suitable match, because the discovery process is entirely fragmented across word-of-mouth recommendations in group chats — with no visibility into tutor availability, current rates, or subject specialisation — causing them to either make a poor match on the first available option or give up on finding a tutor before an exam. Currently they cope by posting requests in multiple WhatsApp and Messenger group chats, collecting names from replies, and contacting each name individually to check availability, rate, and schedule fit. This workaround is inadequate because information is stale by the time it reaches the student, each tutor contact is a separate manual negotiation, and the entire process restarts if the first option falls through.

---

## Breakdown

**Who has the problem (specific user segment):**
University students in Georgia — particularly first, second, and third-year students at KIU and similar institutions — who are actively searching for a private tutor in a specific subject at the start of a semester or in the weeks before exams.

---

**What exactly goes wrong (the pain):**
Students cannot see tutor availability, current rates, or subject specialisation in one place. Every piece of information requires a separate conversation. By the time a student contacts a tutor via a forwarded recommendation, the tutor may already be fully booked, may have changed their rate, or may not actually cover the specific topics the student needs. The student has no way to know this before initiating contact.

---

**When and where it happens (context):**
At the start of each semester and in the 4–6 weeks before major exams, when demand for tutors spikes simultaneously across the student population. The problem is most acute on mobile, inside WhatsApp and Messenger, where the student is posting and following up on multiple parallel threads.

---

**What it costs them (consequence):**
Students spend between several hours and a full week on a search that should take minutes. Students who cannot find a tutor quickly enough either go without — which compounds into worse academic outcomes — or settle for a tutor who is not the right fit for their subject or learning level. Nino spent close to a week searching for one subject tutor this semester. Two tutors were already full, one had raised their rate, and one taught at times that conflicted with lectures.

---

**What they do about it today (current workaround):**
Students post a request in 2–4 WhatsApp or Messenger group chats (course groups, year groups, accommodation groups). They collect names from replies, then message each tutor individually on a separate chat to ask about availability, rate, and subject coverage. If the first match fails, they repeat from the beginning. Some students ask a specific trusted friend to make a direct introduction, which shortens the process but is only available to students with the right social connections.

---

**Why the workaround is inadequate:**
Information goes stale between the time a recommendation is made and the time the student acts on it. There is no shared visibility — two students searching for the same tutor on the same day have no way to know the tutor is already in two simultaneous conversations. The process is entirely serial and manual: each failure means starting over, not narrowing down. Students with smaller social networks — particularly international students or students new to KIU — have no starting point at all.
