## Script Purpose

This script is a guide, not a rigid questionnaire. Follow threads that the interviewee opens. The goal is to learn, not to complete a checklist. Every question passes the Mom Test: past-tense, open-ended, no solution references, no leading language.

---

## Screener Questions (Confirm Before Scheduling)

1. Have you searched for a private tutor at any point since starting university?
2. Was it for a specific subject or course rather than general academic support?
3. Tell me a bit about how that search went. *(Listen for any sign of difficulty — if they say "fine, I found someone immediately," check whether they match the ICP before proceeding.)*

---

## Interview Introduction

"Thank you for making the time — I really appreciate it. I am working on a university research project where we are trying to understand how students find tutors when they need help with a subject. I am not selling anything and there is nothing to sign up for. I just want to understand what the experience has actually been like for you. There are no right or wrong answers.

Do you mind if I take notes while we talk?"

---

## Phase 1: Opening and Context (3 minutes)

**Goal:** Get the participant into storytelling mode quickly. Move past pleasantries within the first two questions.

**Question 1:**
Walk me through the last time you looked for a private tutor. Where did you start and what did you do?

*What you are listening for:* The first channel they tried, whether they had a starting point at all, and how quickly the search encountered its first obstacle.

*Follow-up probe:* "And then what happened?"

---

## Phase 2: Problem Exploration (10 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand the failure modes in sequence — not a general summary of frustration, but a step-by-step account of what went wrong and what happened at each step.

**Question 2:**
When a contact did not work out — they were full, or did not reply, or the rate was wrong — what did you do in that moment?

*What you are listening for:* Whether the student returns to the same channel, looks for a new one, or gives up. The restart dynamic.

*Follow-up probe:* "Walk me through that specifically — what did you do right after you found out it was not going to work?"

---

**Question 3:**
By the end of the search, how many people had you contacted before finding someone — or before stopping?

*What you are listening for:* A concrete number that is comparable across interviews. Also the ratio of contacts to outcomes.

---

**Question 4:**
What happened in the course or subject while you were still searching?

*What you are listening for:* The compounding cost. Did they fall further behind? Did they miss an assessment? Did they change their study approach to compensate?

*Follow-up probe:* "And how did that affect things for you?"

---

**Question 5:**
How did it feel by the end of the process?

*What you are listening for:* Emotional tone — resignation, frustration, relief if they found someone, regret if they did not. This is the most important question for understanding pain intensity.

---

## Phase 3: Problem Deep Dive (10 minutes)

**Goal:** Surface the specific information gaps and trust requirements that make the search difficult.

**Question 6:**
Was there anything you wanted to know about a tutor before contacting them that you could not find out without messaging them directly?

*What you are listening for:* Rate, availability, subject specialisation, quality/credibility signals. Which of these felt most important?

---

**Question 7:**
When you did find someone you were willing to work with — what made you trust that this was the right person?

*What you are listening for:* The trust mechanism. Personal vouching? Prior knowledge? Their own feeling after one session? This question surfaces the conversion criteria.

*Follow-up probe:* "Would you have felt the same if someone had just given you their name without any other context?"

---

**Question 8:**
Have you ever tried to find a tutor through anything other than personal contacts — a website, an app, anything like that? What did you find?

*What you are listening for:* Whether they searched for a structured solution, what they found, and why it did not work for their context.

---

## Phase 4: Current Solutions (5 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand the full inventory of channels used and why each one was or was not adequate.

**Question 9:**
Which channels or methods did you actually use — group chats, asking specific people, anything else? Walk me through all of them.

---

**Question 10:**
For each one — how well did it work, and what was specifically missing from it?

*What you are listening for:* The gap between what the channel provided and what the student actually needed.

---

## Phase 4B: Tutor Variant (use only when interviewing active private tutors)

*Replace Phase 4 with these questions when the participant is an active tutor rather than a student searcher.*

**Question 9T:**
When students contact you looking for a tutor — what does that experience look like for you?

**Question 10T:**
When you have to say no to a student, what happens next — for them and for you?

**Question 11T:**
Have you ever had to turn down a student you would have liked to take, because of timing or a schedule clash?

**Question 12T:**
If you could change one thing about how students find you, what would it be?

---

## Phase 5: Close (3 minutes)

**Question 11 (or 13 for tutor variant):**
Is there anything about searching for tutors — or about how tutoring works at KIU more generally — that I have not asked about but that you think is important?

**Referral question:**
Do you know anyone else who has been through a difficult tutor search, or who is looking for a tutor right now? Would you be willing to introduce me — even just by forwarding my number?

**Close:**
This has been really useful — thank you. If I have a quick follow-up question this week, is it okay to message you?

---

## Pre-Interview Checklist

- [ ] Read the full script 10 minutes before starting
- [ ] Notes document open and ready
- [ ] Confirm whether participant is a student searcher or active tutor — use Phase 4 or 4B accordingly
- [ ] All questions ask about the past, not the future
- [ ] No mention of any solution or product idea
- [ ] If the participant asks what you are building: "We are still in the research phase — we want to understand the problem before deciding what to build."
