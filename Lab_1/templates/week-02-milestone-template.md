## Problem Pool Summary

**Total problems generated:** 12  
**Number of team members who contributed individual problems:** 4 / 4  

**Domains represented in the pool:**  
Tutor discovery, session quality and continuity, progress tracking and visibility, tutor pricing, scheduling logistics, exam preparation alignment

**Honest team reflection on the brainstorming session:**  
The silent brainstorming phase was harder than expected — several team members initially drafted problems that were too broad ("tutors have trouble with communication") and had to push themselves toward more specific observations. The requirement to trace every problem back to a personal observation was the most useful constraint: it forced us away from problems we had read about or assumed, and toward things we had actually seen. The round-robin sharing round revealed that our problems clustered around two distinct pain points — the logistics burden on tutors, and the lack of visibility and accountability for students and parents. We were careful not to resolve this tension during the sharing round. Both directions remain open for Lab 2.

## Two Candidate Problems (for Lab 2 prep)

**Candidate A:** Independent private tutors spend 30–45 minutes per week managing scheduling over WhatsApp and are turning down new students because of the overhead (Lizi — Heartburn)

**Candidate B:** Parents paying for private tutoring have no reliable way to assess whether sessions are producing progress before a disappointing test result arrives (Lizi — Headache)

**What makes these two stand out from the others in the pool?**  
Both are grounded in direct observation with named, specific consequences. Candidate A has a clear financial cost (turned-down students = lost income) that gives it strong Four Filters potential on the "significant pain" criterion. Candidate B has a strong emotional driver (parental anxiety about a child's education) and is supported by observations from three different team members independently, which suggests the problem is not isolated. Both are reachable: we have personal connections to tutors and to parents of tutored students in our immediate networks.

Importantly, these two candidates represent opposite sides of the tutor-student relationship. Selecting one in Lab 2 will meaningfully shape the direction of our product — we cannot build for both without losing focus.
