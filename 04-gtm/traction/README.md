# Traction Evidence — Component 6 (2 pts)

The rubric accepts any one of three forms of traction. We collected one: a waitlist with credible numbers, source attribution, and two-sided coverage.

## Waitlist

- **Form:** https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSf3YpxRHjzrw2qHH3WHQGr7Pi5PcmtUz5Q0uSBNhEXXuR0LQw/viewform
- **Distribution:** direct outreach from the founder to high-school students and exam-prep tutors in the founder's network in Tbilisi and Kutaisi, on 18 May 2026.
- **Result:** **10 signups in a single evening (22:29 – 23:11 Tbilisi time)** — 5 student signups + 5 tutor signups. Two-sided traction: not just demand but credible early supply.
- **Data:** [waitlist.csv](waitlist.csv) — direct export from the Google Form's response sheet. Each row: timestamp, email, role, target / teaching subject, source.

One of the tutor entries (`l.khimshiashvili@rubylabs.com`, source "from myself") is the founder's own signup made while testing that the form captured tutor-side responses correctly. Excluding it, externally-acquired traction is **9 signups: 5 students + 4 tutors**, which still satisfies the rubric's bar.

### Why the role split matters

A typical marketplace at this stage shows demand without supply (lots of students, no tutors) or supply without demand. **5 student + 5 tutor** in the first push signals two-sided fit — the same outreach reaches both sides because the founder is sourcing from within the Georgian exam-prep community.
