# Interview Script v1

**Team Name:** TutorLink Team
**Problem this script is for:** University students in Georgia spend significantly more time and effort finding a suitable private tutor than the task should require, because the discovery process is entirely fragmented across word-of-mouth channels with no visibility into availability, rates, or subject specialisation.
**ICP this script targets:** Primary — university students at KIU who have recently searched for or are currently searching for a private tutor
**Version:** v1.0
**Date created:** March 20, 2026
**Created by:** Nino Tsutskiridze, Lizi Margvelashvili, Luka Khimshiashvili, Mari Janjghava

---

## Script Purpose

This script is a guide, not a rigid questionnaire. Use it to structure the conversation but follow interesting threads when the interviewee takes you somewhere unexpected. The goal is to learn, not to complete a checklist.

Every question in this script passes the Mom Test:
- Questions ask about past behaviour, not future intentions
- Questions ask about the problem, not about a solution
- No leading questions
- No hypotheticals ("would you use…?")

---

## Screener Questions (Confirm Before Scheduling)

1. Have you searched for a private tutor at any point since starting university?
2. Was it for a specific subject or course, rather than general academic support?
3. Did you find the search process easy or difficult?

If they answer yes to 1 and 2, and describe any difficulty in 3, they match the ICP. Proceed with scheduling.

---

## Interview Introduction (Read once, then speak naturally — do not read this word-for-word in the interview)

"Thank you for making the time — I really appreciate it. I'm doing research for a university project where we're trying to understand how students find tutors when they need help with a subject. I'm not selling anything and there's no right or wrong answer to anything I'll ask. I just want to understand what the experience has actually been like for you. Is that okay?

Do you mind if I take notes while we talk?"

---

## Phase 1: Opening and Context (5 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand this person's general academic situation and relationship with tutoring before focusing on the search experience.

**Question 1:**
Tell me a bit about your current semester — what subjects are you taking and how has it been going?

**Question 2:**
Have you ever worked with a private tutor since starting university? What led you to that?

**Follow-up prompts:**
- "Can you tell me more about that?"
- "How did that come about?"
- "When was this?"

---

## Phase 2: Problem Exploration (10 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand the search experience in broad strokes before going deep. Let them describe what happened before directing them to the specific friction points.

**Question 3:**
Walk me through the last time you looked for a tutor. Where did you start and what did you do?

**Question 4:**
What were the most time-consuming or frustrating parts of that process?

**Question 5:**
Was there a moment where you felt like you were going in circles, or had to start over? Tell me about that.

**Follow-up prompts:**
- "What happened next?"
- "How long did that take?"
- "How did that make you feel at the time?"

---

## Phase 3: Problem Deep Dive (15 minutes)

**Goal:** Get a step-by-step reconstruction of the actual search behaviour and understand the specific friction at each stage.

**Question 6:**
Take me through what you actually did, step by step, from the moment you decided you needed a tutor to the moment you either found one or stopped looking. Be as specific as you can.

*[Listen for: where they started, how many people they contacted, how many failed, what information was missing, how long each step took, what caused them to restart.]*

**Question 7:**
When you contacted a tutor who turned out not to work out — too expensive, already full, wrong subject — what did you do next? How did that affect the rest of the search?

**Question 8:**
Was there anything you wanted to know about a tutor before contacting them that you couldn't find out without messaging them directly? What was that?

**Question 9:**
How long did the whole search take, from deciding you needed a tutor to actually starting sessions? Does that feel like a normal amount of time or more than it should have been?

**Question 10:**
Think about the students you know who found a tutor quickly. How did they do it? What did they have that made it easier?

*[This question surfaces the difference between students with strong social networks and those without — a key ICP dimension.]*

---

## Phase 4: Current Solutions (10 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand exactly which tools and channels they used and why each one was or wasn't adequate.

**Question 11:**
Which platforms or channels did you actually use during the search — group chats, specific apps, websites, asking particular people? Walk me through all of them.

**Question 12:**
For each one you tried — how well did it work, and what was missing from it?

**Question 13:**
Have you ever seen anyone else search for a tutor in a way that worked better than what you did? What were they doing differently?

---

## Phase 5: Close (5 minutes)

**Question 14:**
Is there anything about searching for tutors — or about how tutoring works at KIU more generally — that I haven't asked about but that you think is important?

**Referral question:**
Do you know anyone else who has been through a frustrating tutor search recently, or who is looking for a tutor right now? Would you be okay to introduce me — even just by forwarding my number?

**Close:**
This has been genuinely useful — thank you. If I have a quick follow-up question later this week, is it okay to message you?
