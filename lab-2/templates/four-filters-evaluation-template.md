## Scoring Reference

For each filter, score 1 to 3:
- **3** = Clear evidence this passes
- **2** = Possible pass, but uncertain — needs more information
- **1** = Fails this filter

| Filter | What it measures |
|--------|-----------------|
| Real and Recurring | Happens often enough to justify building a solution |
| Painful Enough to Act | People are already trying to solve it (workarounds exist) |
| Underserved Today | Existing solutions are genuinely inadequate |
| Accessible to You | You can interview users this week and build for this space |

## Evaluations

### Problem 1: Students struggle to find affordable and reliable housing in Tbilisi

**Real and Recurring**
- Score: 3/3  
- Justification: This problem occurs every semester for many students. Several team members have personally experienced it or know multiple people who faced difficulties finding housing.

**Painful Enough to Act**
- Score: 3/3  
- Known workaround: Students search Facebook groups, rental websites, and ask friends or classmates.  
- Justification: Students actively spend significant time and effort trying to solve this problem, showing strong pain.

**Underserved Today**
- Score: 2/3  
- Existing solutions: Facebook Marketplace, local rental websites, personal networks  
- Justification: These solutions exist but are unreliable, unstructured, and often contain outdated or misleading listings.

**Accessible to You**
- Score: 3/3  
- Who could you interview this week: KIU students, friends, classmates, people currently renting  
- Justification: The team has direct access to many students who match this problem.

**Total: 11/12**  
**Recommendation:** Strong candidate

## Team Discussion Notes

The main discussion was between the housing problem and the LMS timetable issue. Some team members initially preferred the LMS problem because it is simpler and easier to prototype. However, others argued that the housing problem has a much stronger real-world impact, especially financially and emotionally for students.

There was also a discussion about feasibility, since housing is a broader and more complex problem. To address this, the team agreed to narrow the focus to affordable and reliable listings rather than solving the entire housing system.

The final decision was made based on the higher level of pain and importance of the housing problem, while still keeping LMS as a backup option.

---

## Decision

Our committed problem for Lab 2 is:

**University students in Tbilisi struggle to find affordable and reliable rental housing before the start of each semester.**

This problem was chosen because it scored highly across all four filters, especially in terms of pain and real-world impact. Students spend significant time and effort searching for housing, often facing high prices, misleading listings, and poor living conditions. The team also has direct access to students experiencing this problem, making it easy to validate through interviews.

The runner-up problem we are keeping alive as our secondary ICP candidate is:

**Students miss lectures due to LMS timetable changes.**
