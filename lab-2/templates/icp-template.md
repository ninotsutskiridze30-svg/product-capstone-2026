## Primary ICP: Tutor Discovery Problem

### 1. Segment

First, second, and third-year university students at KIU or similar Georgian universities who are actively searching for a private tutor in a specific subject. They are searching either at the start of a new semester when they anticipate difficulty in a course, or in the 4–6 weeks before final exams when they realise they have fallen behind. They have a concrete academic need — a specific subject, a specific gap — not a general desire to learn. They are searching independently, without family guidance on how to find tutors in Georgia. Many are studying away from home.

---

### 2. Context

The search happens primarily on mobile, inside WhatsApp and Messenger group chats. The student is at home or in their accommodation, typically in the evening after classes. They know they need help but have no structured way to find it. The search begins with a post in one or more group chats and proceeds as a series of individual conversations with people whose names were forwarded by others. The problem is most intense at semester start and during the exam season (November–December, April–May), when many students are searching simultaneously and the best-known tutors fill up quickly.

---

### 3. Primary Pain

The student cannot see who is available, at what rate, or for which specific topics, without initiating individual conversations that are each a cold approach to a stranger. By the time a recommended tutor is contacted, they may already be full, have changed their rate, or may not teach the specific topics needed. Each failed contact means restarting — not narrowing down. Students with smaller social networks (international students, students new to KIU) have no starting point at all.

---

### 4. Current Workaround

**Step 1:** Post a request in 2–4 WhatsApp or Messenger group chats (course group, year group, accommodation group, faculty group).

**Step 2:** Collect names of tutors forwarded by other students in replies.

**Step 3:** Message each name individually to ask: Are you available? What subjects do you cover? What is your rate? When can you teach?

**Step 4:** If the first match fails (already full, rate too high, schedule conflict), return to the name list or post another request.

**Why it is inadequate:** Information is stale. Two students searching simultaneously for the same tutor have no way to know. The process is entirely manual and serial. Each failure requires restarting rather than filtering. Students with weaker social connections have no initial pool of names to work from.

---

### 5. Job to Be Done

"When I realise I need a tutor for a specific subject, I want to quickly see who is available, what they charge, and what they cover, so I can contact one person and start sessions within a day or two instead of spending a week searching."

---

### 6. Success Signal

The student finds a suitable tutor within the same day they begin searching. They send one or two messages, not twenty. They do not post in group chats asking for recommendations. They begin sessions before the end of the week. Observed behavioural change: the group chat posts stop. The serial individual messaging thread does not start.

---

## Secondary ICP: Parent Progress Visibility Problem

### 1. Segment

Parents or guardians in Tbilisi who are the financial decision-maker for a private tutoring arrangement for a secondary school child aged 12–17. The child is in grades 7–12. The parent is paying for at least one private tutor, typically in mathematics, Georgian language, or English. The tutoring has been running for at least 4–6 weeks — long enough for the problem to have surfaced. The parent is not present during sessions and receives no structured reporting on progress. They work full time and rely on the tutoring arrangement to support their child's academic development independently.

---

### 2. Context

The problem surfaces at two moments. First, at pickup: the tutor gives a brief, always-positive verbal update ("she's doing well, we're making good progress") that the parent cannot meaningfully evaluate. Second, after a disappointing test result: the parent realises the tutoring has not produced the expected progress and has no way to diagnose why. The parent has been paying for weeks or months before this signal arrives.

---

### 3. Primary Pain

The parent is investing a significant recurring expense with no feedback mechanism other than verbal reassurance and eventual test scores. The verbal reassurance is always positive. The test scores arrive with a 2–6 week lag and do not isolate the tutoring contribution from other factors. By the time the parent has evidence that something is wrong, the problem has been developing for weeks. Lizi's neighbour discovered after three months that the tutor had been reteaching the same grammar unit — this information was available at every session but never surfaced.

---

### 4. Current Workaround

**Primary workaround:** Trust the tutor's verbal update at pickup and wait for test results as the only external signal.

**Secondary workaround (minority):** Ask the child what was covered in the session — child gives vague or reconstructed answer. Some parents send a WhatsApp message to the tutor after a bad test result, but this is socially uncomfortable and most avoid it.

**Why it is inadequate:** Verbal updates are non-specific, always positive, and not recorded. Test scores arrive too late to course-correct within a semester. Asking the child is unreliable. Questioning the tutor directly feels accusatory and risks damaging a personal relationship the parent depends on.

---

### 5. Job to Be Done

"When I am paying for my child's private tutoring, I want to see what is actually being covered and whether my child is progressing, so I can make an informed decision about whether to continue with this tutor rather than finding out something is wrong only after months of wasted sessions."

---

### 6. Success Signal

The parent can describe, at any point in the semester, what topics have been covered and what the tutor's assessment of the child's progress is. After a disappointing test result, they have enough information to have a specific, productive conversation with the tutor. They do not discover a problem through a test score alone. Observed behavioural change: the parent stops relying on pickup verbal updates as their only signal.

---
