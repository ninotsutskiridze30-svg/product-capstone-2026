# Usability Test — Script and Protocol

**Goal:** observe whether a real Georgian high-school student (or a tutor of one) can complete the core flow on https://tutoring-lyart.vercel.app without team assistance.

**Format:** intended as a 20-minute moderated session (Zoom screen-share or in person), one participant at a time. The Sprint-1 sessions reported in [usability-findings.md](usability-findings.md) were short informal walkthroughs (10–15 min) rather than the full moderated protocol; this script is the standard the next round of testing will follow.

**Sample target:** 5+ participants — students preparing for the national entrance exams and tutors actively teaching them. Sprint 1 captured 3 sessions against this target; additional sessions planned post-submission.

**Recording:** with explicit verbal consent at the start, audio + screen are recorded for transcription accuracy. Recordings are deleted within 7 days of the findings doc being written.

---

## Script (~20 min total)

### 0. Welcome (2 min)

> "Hi [name], thank you for taking the time. I'm working on a tutoring platform for Georgian high-school exam prep and I want to see if it's easy to use before I show it to more people. I'm going to ask you to try a few things on the website while talking out loud about what you're thinking. There are no right or wrong answers — if anything is confusing, that's *useful* feedback. I'm going to record the screen so I can write down what happened accurately; the recording will be deleted afterwards. Is that OK?"

Wait for verbal yes. Note in findings doc.

### 1. First impression (2 min)

Open https://tutoring-lyart.vercel.app on the participant's device. **Without explaining anything**, ask:

> "What do you think this is? Who do you think it's for? What do you think you can do here?"

Note exact words used.

### 2. Task 1 — Find a tutor (4 min)

> "Imagine you want to find a Math tutor to help you prepare for the entrance exams. Show me how you would do that."

Watch — don't guide. Note every hesitation, click, scroll, and complaint. If they stall completely for >30 seconds, ask "what would you click next if you had to guess?"

### 3. Task 2 — Book a lesson (4 min)

> "OK, you've found a tutor you like. Now book a lesson with them for next week."

Watch the calendar interaction, the confirmation, and whether the user feels the booking succeeded.

### 4. Task 3 — Imagine joining the lesson (3 min)

> "Now imagine the lesson time has arrived. Show me how you would join your lesson."

Watch where they look first (dashboard? email? notification?). The thing they look for first tells us where the entry point *should* be.

### 5. Task 4 — Submit homework / for tutors, create homework (3 min)

For students:
> "After the lesson, your tutor gives you homework. Show me how you would submit your answer."

For tutors:
> "You want to give your student some homework based on what you covered in the lesson. Show me how you would do that."

### 6. Open feedback (2 min)

> "If you could change one thing about this platform, what would it be?"
> "Would you use this yourself? Would you recommend it to a classmate? Why or why not?"

Capture verbatim.

### 7. Wrap (under 1 min)

> "That's it. Thank you. Anything else you want to add?"

End recording.

---

## Consent and data handling

Recording is for note-taking accuracy only. It is not shared with anyone, not uploaded anywhere, and is deleted within 7 days of the findings doc being written. Per-participant findings in [usability-findings.md](usability-findings.md) use pseudonyms; underlying notes naming each participant are held by the founder and available on request.
